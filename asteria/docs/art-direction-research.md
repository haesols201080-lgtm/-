# SUMMARY
The current 3D build doesn't look cheap because it lacks magic props. It looks cheap because its base world isn't believable. Everything is flat-shaded with no textures, no environment reflections and no ambient occlusion (gfx.js mat() defaults to flatShading:true with no maps). Buildings are flat-roofed boxes with parapets and identical lit-at-noon 'sticker' windows, and the few roofs are far too squat. Lighting is even hemisphere light with no warm/cool contrast. Interiors are 20-30 m empty voids lit by a stray street lamp. Colors are saturated toy colors (lime grass, pure blue cones, red checker floor, cyan Tron trim strips). The camera is high with a 58° vertical FOV (about 89° horizontal), which gives a diorama view. Characters are about 4.6 heads tall with no neck. The wizarding-school look the user means comes from a formula of six parts. (1) Heavy, old, very vertical Gothic masonry that shows centuries of additions. (2) Light-and-shadow contrast: warm practical light sources (light from visible fixtures such as candles, lamps and fireplaces) against cool sky or moonlight, strong exposure differences, light shafts and dust. (3) Worn, rich materials: stone, oak, brass, stained glass, velvet, parchment. (4) Dense clutter that makes rooms feel used. (5) Magic that is restrained, has a visible source, serves a purpose and is built into architecture and daily life. (6) An eye-level over-the-shoulder camera that makes buildings loom. As an original direction, Kalidor would be a honey-limestone Gothic academy built on top of a pale, brass-seamed ancient-mage foundation layer. It is crowned with verdigris copper spires, open belfries and armillary instruments. Its magic language is 'resonance': concentric rings, bells, tuning-fork pinnacles. The findings below give implementable rules: numeric proportions, luminance and contrast targets, procedural material recipes, hex palettes for 8 scenes, rules for spell effects, ambient magic, characters, camera and UI, a do-not list, IP guardrails, and a 'build one finished scene first, with acceptance tests' process. Web pages could not be fetched (DNS failure on every host), so the references come from search-result summaries plus model knowledge. npm checks confirm that free (CC0) HDRI sky/lighting images and normal maps (@pmndrs/assets 1.7.0) and three@0.186.1 ambient-occlusion and depth-of-field passes can be obtained. No usable character or building models exist on npm, so those must be built procedurally.

## DIAGNOSIS: 14 specific reasons the current 3D build reads as cheap [must]
Seen in 3d_open2/open1, 3d_dorm, 3d_hall, grid.png, 3d_title_bg, 3d_cathedral, 3d_prima, and confirmed in the code. (1) SHADING: gfx.js mat() builds MeshStandardMaterial with flatShading defaulting to true and no map, normalMap or roughnessMap, so every face is one flat sRGB color. That is the 'untextured prototype' look. Only the terrain gets a 256px noise texture. (2) NO IMAGE-BASED LIGHTING OR AO: there is no PMREM envMap, so brass (metal 0.7) and glass render dead gray. There is no SSAO/GTAO, so props and walls float with no contact darkening at seams. (3) FLAT LIGHT: HemisphereLight 0.75 plus a high midday sun at 2.2 gives even values everywhere. There is no raking golden-hour light and no warm/cool split. Interiors light 20-30 m rooms with 1-2 pooled PointLights (range 14), giving either one light pool in a gray void (dorm) or uniform cyan haze (dungeons). (4) ARCHITECTURE (scene.js facade()): every building is a box with a FLAT ROOF, a parapet and a cornice slab, so the skyline is flat. The only spikes are a clock-tower pyramid (height/diameter about 0.7) and gate cones (about 1.1), both far too squat. Windows are identical 1.1x1.6 m GLOW MeshBasic rectangles every 3.2 m, glowing at noon, so they read as stickers rather than deep openings. There is no plinth, string course, buttress, recess or visible wall thickness. (5) TOY PALETTE: lime greens #5fae4a/#4f9a42/#6ab84a, pure-blue cones #4a5a9a, a red checker floor in the hall, cyan everywhere. (6) GROUND: a checkerboard tile plaza at uniform scale, with no dirt, gravel or moss where walls meet the ground. (7) SCALE: the plaza and dorm are 3-4x too large for the people. The dorm is about 30 m of empty floor. The hall is a red floor under a night-sky dome with one bench, no visible walls, tables or candles (the star-sky ceiling is also an IP risk). (8) LIGHTS WITHOUT FIXTURES: an outdoor street lamp stands in the dorm, and glowing balls float. (9) DUNGEONS: all 12 interiors use one recipe: flat floor, low walls with glowing cyan trim strips (sci-fi/Tron), monochrome cyan fog, no ornament. (10) WORLD: lollipop trees, inverted-cone 'ice cream' floating islands, laser-bright ley-line beams across daytime fields, and white fog that lowers contrast across the whole title frame instead of creating depth layers. (11) CAMERA: PerspectiveCamera(58) vertical is about 89° horizontal at 16:9. With the high oblique placement this gives an RTS/diorama view. In town, characters are 10-15% of frame height and buildings never loom. (12) CHARACTER (actors.js makeRig): about 2.07 m tall with a 0.42 m sphere head, so about 4.6 heads tall (chibi). There is no neck (the head floats above the collar), the torso is a cylinder, limbs are straight tubes, eyes are black dots, the cape is a rigid open cone and the scarf is a torus. (13) POST-PROCESSING: bloom threshold 0.92, saturation boost 1.08, one global grade, no exposure adaptation between inside and outside. (14) UI: modern rounded navy pills and cards in IBM Plex Sans KR, a mobile-app look that breaks the period fantasy. CONCLUSION: the base reality is wrong. Adding more flying props cannot fix it.

## CORE THESIS: what makes a wizarding-school game read as a 'magical world' [must]
Magic reads as magic only when it is the exception, lit against a heavy, old, believable world. RULE 85/15: about 85% of every frame is grounded and believable (masonry, timber, cloth, books, weather, people doing ordinary things). About 15% is magic, and magic emissive surfaces should be at most 10-15% of screen pixels. The six pillars: (P1) AGE AND HISTORY IN THE ARCHITECTURE. A 1000-year academy shows accretion: older round-arched lower parts, Gothic upper parts, later repairs, wear, soot, moss. The film's production designer described Hogwarts as a deliberate mix of Gothic periods because it is 1000 years old, with real cathedral profiles exaggerated to taller, pointier spires for drama. (P2) EXTREME VERTICALITY AND A JAGGED SKYLINE seen from human eye level. (P3) LIGHT-AND-SHADOW CONTRAST: warm practical lights (candle, fire, lantern) against cool sky or moonlight, deep shadows, light shafts with dust, glossy floors that reflect warm light. Avalanche's lighting team used physically based light values with global auto-exposure and auto white balance. Their GDC 2024 rendering talk covered rendering with hundreds of shadow-casting lights plus indoor/outdoor transitions, time of day, exposure, seasons and weather. So lighting density and exposure contrast are core to the look, not polish. (P4) MATERIAL RICHNESS: stone, dark oak, brass, stained glass, slate, velvet, parchment, each with wear. (P5) LIVED-IN SCHOLARLY CLUTTER that implies people practice magic as study and craft: books, scrolls, instruments, bottles, maps, trunks. (P6) MAGIC THAT IS EMBEDDED: it has a source (a rune or conduit), a purpose (it does a job) and a keeper (someone made or maintains it), and it reacts to the player. Flying whales and books are seasoning, at most 2 airborne magic items per frame, and they must have a reason to be there. The game's concept art describes ancient spaces as 'magic shaping raw materials into organic Gothic designs'. Use that principle for the ancient-mage engines, as opposed to the human-cut masonry of the academy.

## PROCESS: build finished 'beauty corners' first and gate everything on acceptance tests [must]
Stop expanding content. Before touching any dungeon, build 3 HERO FRAMES to final quality and get user approval: (H1) the Kalidor approach at golden hour from a stone bridge, with academy skyline and town; (H2) the Great Court quad at dusk with students, lit windows and ley conduits; (H3) the Refectory or Library interior at evening with candles and window shafts. Then turn the approved pieces into a reusable kit. ACCEPTANCE TESTS for every screenshot: (1) SQUINT/GRAYSCALE: 3 clear value groups. In interiors at least 15% of pixels are dark (sRGB value under 30) and 1-5% are highlights (over 240). (2) SILHOUETTE: an all-black skyline render still reads as 'castle': at least 7 distinct vertical spikes, no two adjacent tower tops at the same height, no unbroken roofline longer than 15 m. (3) WARM/COOL: at least one warm source (1800-3500K) and one cool source (sky, moon or mana) is visible. (4) NO NAKED SURFACE: every surface has at least 2 detail frequencies, macro variation (2-10 m) and micro detail (1-30 cm normal or albedo). (5) EDGES: no unbeveled 90° edge within 10 m of the camera. (6) PROP DENSITY: interiors show at least 60 prop instances per frame, exteriors at least 30 (see the interiors finding). (7) MOTIVATED LIGHT: every light has a visible fixture or opening. (8) MAGIC BUDGET: 1 hero magic element, 2-4 embedded ambient effects, at most 2 airborne items. (9) SCALE CUE: a door, a person or a stair is visible. (10) Compare side by side with a private mood-board image of a similar shot from the reference game (internal only, never shipped).

## ARCHITECTURE 1: site and silhouette rules for Kalidor [must]
SITE (original, not a castle on a black lake): Kalidor crowns a terraced limestone hill at the fork of a river gorge, over the ley-line confluence. The academy precincts are split across two spurs and linked by (a) a multi-arched stone viaduct, 5-7 round arches, piers 1/4 of the span, and (b) a covered timber bridge. The town wraps the lower slopes in terraces of steep-roofed houses. Waterfalls fall into the gorge. SKYLINE IN 3 TIERS: tier 1 is the big masses (refectory hall and library with steep 55° roofs). Tier 2 is 5-9 towers of different heights, heights in roughly a 1 : 1.3 : 1.7 : 2.2 : 3 progression, with the Resonance Bell Tower as the tallest landmark, visible from most of the continent like Breath of the Wild's castle. Tier 3 is pinnacles, chimneys, finials, dormers and stair turrets breaking every ridge. ASYMMETRY: no mirrored facades at the precinct scale. Each wing differs in height, roof type and window rhythm, and wings meet at irregular angles following the terrain. ACCRETION: the ground stratum is the ancient-mage layer. Above it are 'early academy' parts (round arches, small deep windows, thick walls), then 'high academy' Gothic (pointed arches, big tracery windows, buttresses), then a few later Tudor-arch additions and timber-framed annexes. Different eras use slightly different stone tones. VIEW DESIGN: every major approach path ends in a framed vista of the bell tower (through an arch, between trees, across the gorge). Exaggerate real-world verticality by about 1.3-1.5x, as the films did with taller, pointier spires.

## ARCHITECTURE 2: numeric proportions and the procedural Gothic kit [must]
GRID: 1 m base unit, 6 m structural bay (buttress to buttress). Story heights: academic halls 5 m, residences 3.5 m, town houses 3 m. WALL DEPTH: openings show 0.9-1.5 m of wall thickness on the castle and 0.5 m in town, and window reveals are at least 0.4 m deep so every window throws a shadow line. WINDOWS: lancets 1.2-1.8 m wide and 3-5x as tall, ground sill at 1.0-1.2 m, hall clerestory sill at 4-5 m. Pair them under a containing arch with a quatrefoil or oculus between the heads. Use at least 3 window types per building. At dusk light only 30-50% of windows, each with its own warmth (#FFB866 to #FF9A4A) and intensity (±30%). By day use dark glass (#1E2430, roughness 0.15, reflecting the env map) with lead mullions, never emissive. ARCHES: equilateral pointed arch (radius = span, apex 0.866 x span above the springing point) as the standard. Lancet (radius 1.3-1.6 x span) for towers and the chapel. Semicircular only in the oldest stratum. Four-centred Tudor arches for late doors. MAIN PORTAL: 3.5-5 m wide, 6-9 m tall, 3-5 recessed concentric arch rings 0.3 m deep each, with a rune keystone. ROOFS: gable pitch 50-60°. Conical tower roofs 1.6-2.5x as tall as their base diameter, with flared eaves (a 10-15° kick) and a 0.6-1.5 m brass finial. Square-tower spires at least 2x as tall as they are wide. Dormers every 2 bays on residential wings. NOTHING HAS A FLAT ROOF except walkable terraces with balustrades. TOWERS: body height 3-5x width, round towers 5-9 m in diameter. Stair turrets 2.5-3.5 m in diameter on corners, rising 3-6 m above the parapet. At least one corbelled overhanging upper stage (0.5-1 m) per massing group. BUTTRESSES: 1.0-1.6 m deep, 0.8-1.2 m wide, one per bay, with 2-3 set-backs of 0.25-0.4 m each under sloped caps, topped by 3-5 m pinnacles. Flying buttresses only on the refectory and the salt-coast cathedral. BATTLEMENTS: only on the gatehouse, curtain walls and defensive towers, never on academic roofs. Merlons 0.8-1.0 m wide and 1.0-1.2 m tall, gaps 0.5-0.6 m, carried on a projecting corbel table 0.3-0.5 m out. MOULDINGS: plinth 0.6-1.0 m tall, projecting 0.15-0.3 m, with a sloped top; a string course (0.2-0.3 m) at every floor line; a corbel table under the eaves; a drip mould over every window. RULE OF PLANES: no flat wall field bigger than 6x6 m without an opening, a buttress, a change of plane of at least 0.2 m, or ornament. BEVELS: chamfer every block-like piece 2-6 cm so edges catch highlights. CODE CONSTRUCTION: THREE.Shape with absarc for pointed and round arches, then ExtrudeGeometry with bevel for frames, arcades and tracery (holes cut into the shape). LatheGeometry for round towers, flared cones, columns, balusters and finials. RoundedBoxGeometry for ashlar pieces. Merge each module with BufferGeometryUtils and instance repeated modules (window bay, buttress, merlon, pinnacle, dormer). LOD: full geometry within 30 m, normal and texture detail from 30-150 m, merged silhouette meshes beyond 150 m, keeping all spikes. INTERIOR STRUCTURE: ribbed vaults over square bays with 0.3 m ribs springing from clustered columns. Hammerbeam timber roofs for halls. Spiral stairs in turrets: radius 1.2-1.6 m, 16-20 steps per turn, central newel column, slit windows.

## INTERIORS 1: clutter density rules and the prop library [must]
SCALE FIRST: rooms are sized for people (see the per-room numbers). An empty 30 m dorm is the biggest current error. DENSITY RULES: (1) Every 2-3 m of wall has something: a window, tapestry, banner, memory-glass panel, shelf, sconce, fireplace or niche statue. (2) Clutter gradient: dense along walls and in corners, sparse on walking paths, which stay 1.2-2 m clear. (3) Triads: props cluster as large + medium + small. ## LIGHTING 1: principles, contrast ratios and measurable luminance targets [must]
RULES: (1) Every light is MOTIVATED: sun, sky, moon, window, candle, fire, lantern or a spell. No unexplained fill. (2) One dominant key per scene. Fill is cooler and at most 1/4 of the key (exterior) or 1/10 (interior). Ambient or hemisphere light is at most 10-15% of the key. (3) Warm against cool is always present. (4) Large dark masses are allowed and wanted: shadows hold mood, and detail can disappear in them. KEY:FILL: golden-hour exterior 4:1 to 6:1, overcast or noon 3:1, night exterior 8:1 (moon vs. shadow) with lantern pools reaching day-like values locally, candlelit interior 12:1 to 20:1. COLOR TEMPERATURES (hex for three.js light colors): candle 1900K #FFA24A (flame core #FFE2A0), fireplace 1800K #FF7A30, oil lamp or lantern 2400K #FFB060, golden-hour sun 3200K #FFBE7E, noon sun 5500K #FFF1E0, sky fill #8FB3E6 to #C9D9FF, moonlight (film convention) #9DB4FF, mana and ley #7FF0FF. LUMINANCE TARGETS after tone mapping, measured as sRGB 0-255 values on screenshots. Golden-hour exterior: sunlit limestone 200-225, shadowed stone 70-100 tinted blue-violet, sky near the sun 245+, zenith about 140, lit windows 230+. Candlelit interior: wall next to a sconce 170-200, mid-room 60-90, corners 10-25, flames and windows at 250 with bloom. Night exterior: moonlit stone 55-80 (blue), shadow 10-20, windows 230+, lantern pool on stone 160-200. EXPOSURE ADAPTATION: interiors use about +1.5 to +2.5 EV exposure compared with exterior day. Lerp exposure over 1.2-1.8 s on entering or leaving, so doorways bloom bright from inside and look dark from outside, as with the reference game's auto-exposure. Use per-zone white balance so candlelit rooms read warm but not orange mush.

DEFAULT HERO TIME is golden hour, 16:00-17:30. Sun elevation 12-22°, azimuth set to rake main facades at 30-50° so mouldings and buttresses throw long shadows. Sun color #FFBE7E, sky fill #7F9CCF, the shadow side reads blue-violet (#3A3F5C influence). Use a Preetham Sky or the existing gradient sky with Mie forward-scatter glow around the sun (#F3D2A6 horizon) and layered clouds: a cumulus billboard ring plus a high cirrus noise dome lit warm on the sun side and lavender on the shade side. DAY (10-15h): sun #FFF1E0 at 45-60°, keep a little haze and cloud shadows (a moving noise texture in the light cookie or terrain shader). Lower saturation slightly. DUSK (blue hour): sun under the horizon, sky #263258 to #F0A070 at the horizon, 30-50% of windows lit, lanterns switch on in a wave (a magic touch: a lamplighter spell ripples down the street). NIGHT: moon key #9DB4FF high from one side (shadow-casting), stars, faint aurora only in the mountain region. Ley conduits and lanterns become the main readable lights. Valley fog in the gorge #2A3456. WEATHER AND SEASONS add value cheaply: rain darkens and glosses stone (roughness 0.85 down to 0.35, albedo x0.75), puddles reflect windows. Snow caps on upward faces use the same normal-up mask as moss.

## LIGHTING 3: how to achieve candle-dense interiors in three.js within budget [must]
WebGL forward rendering cannot afford hundreds of real lights, so fake the density. (1) BAKE STATIC LIGHT AT LOAD TIME: for each interior, compute per-vertex or lightmap irradiance from all static candles, sconces, fireplaces and windows (inverse-square falloff, color, plus a simple occlusion ray or none) and AO, stored in a second vertex-color attribute or a low-res lightMap (aoMap/lightMap need a uv2 in r147). Subdivide floors and walls enough to hold vertex lighting: about 0.5 m vertex spacing near lights. (2) REAL DYNAMIC LIGHTS: pool 6-8 PointLights on desktop and 2-3 on mobile, assigned each frame to the nearest or most important sources: flickering fires and candles near the camera plus spell lights. Only 1 shadow-casting interior light at a time: the window or sun spot, or a fireplace on desktop. (3) GLOW CARDS: each candle flame is a small additive billboard (HDR value 4-8) plus a soft halo sprite (radius 0.3-0.6 m, 10-20% opacity) and a warm additive decal on the wall above it. That gives the 'many lights' look for free. (4) FLICKER: intensity times 1 + 0.08·noise(t·8 + id) for candles and 0.15 for fire, with a slight position jitter on fire. (5) WINDOW SHAFTS: see the atmosphere finding. ## WHAT NOT TO DO (things that make it look cheap) [must]
(1) Flat vertex colors with no texture, normal or roughness variation; flatShading on anything except crystals and ice. (2) Unbeveled box edges, boxes as buildings, FLAT ROOFS, parapets on academic halls. (3) Squat roofs (cone height under 1.6x diameter, gable pitch under 45°). (4) Identical evenly spaced windows; emissive windows at noon; windows flush with the wall (no reveal). (5) Uniform lighting: strong hemisphere or ambient fill, high noon as the default, no shadow pockets, no warm/cool contrast. (6) Light sources without fixtures (glowing balls, a street lamp indoors). (7) Saturated toy colors: lime greens, pure primary blues and reds, checkerboard floors, neon cyan as the base color of whole rooms. (8) Sci-fi glowing trim strips (Tron lines) in ancient or Gothic spaces. (9) Oversized empty rooms and plazas; bare floors over 4x4 m; fewer than about 60 props per interior frame. (10) Whiteout fog that lowers contrast across the whole frame; one constant fog color. (11) Lollipop trees, uniform tree color, inverted-cone floating islands, laser-beam ley lines by day. (12) Big-head capsule characters, dot eyes, no neck, rigid cone capes, torus scarves. (13) A high, wide-FOV diorama camera in gameplay. (14) Modern app UI (rounded navy pills, sans-serif body text, emoji-like hearts). (15) Magic as random flying props or constant particle spam; more than 3 saturated hues on screen; magic emissive above 15% of the frame. (16) Perfect symmetry and grid alignment; no grime at wall bases; no wear on stairs or floors. (17) Pure black (#000) shadows or pure white (#FFF) surfaces; every value should carry some hue.
